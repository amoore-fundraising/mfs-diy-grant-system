# Theory of Change

Help the organization put into writing how its work creates change. The Theory of Change feeds the Master Grant Narrative, every proposal's outcomes section, and every grant report.

Apply organization-profile (if a profile exists) and grant-guardrails throughout.

## Who should be in the room

This works best when the executive director answers, ideally with one or two program leaders. Tell the user this at the start. The answers should sound like the people who do the work.

## Inputs

- The interview (Step 1). Required. A theory of change must come from the organization's own words.
- Anything the user shares: strategic plan, logic model or existing theory of change, annual or impact report, program data, a recording transcript or notes from a planning session.

If the user shares a transcript or notes from a session where they already discussed these questions, use it and ask only about what is missing.

## The framework

| Part | Elements |
| --- | --- |
| Need | Problem, people most affected, root causes, purpose and origin, what happens without the organization |
| Work | Inputs, programs grouped into two to four pathways, partners, behaviors to shift, how change happens, the Big Idea |
| Results | Outputs, outcomes (short-term, intermediate, long-term), systems influence, Pathway to Impact, 10-year vision |

## Step 1: Interview

Ask in five short rounds. Wait for answers before moving on. Encourage stories and plain words; there are no wrong answers.

**Round 1: The need**

1. What problem does your organization exist to solve?
2. Who is most affected by it?
3. What causes it? What is underneath the problem?
4. What would happen to the people you serve if your organization were not here?

**Round 2: Who you are**

5. How did the organization start, and how has its purpose changed over time?

**Round 3: Your work**

6. What are your programs? Which ones belong together because they create the same kind of change? These groups become your pathways.
7. Who are your key partners, and what do they do?
8. What do you need to do the work well: staff, volunteers, space, funding, relationships?
9. In one or two sentences, how does your work create change? This becomes your Big Idea.

**Round 4: Results**

10. For each pathway: what do you deliver and count? (These are outputs.)
11. What changes for people within 6 to 12 months? In 1 to 3 years? In the long run? (These are outcomes.)
12. What do you measure today, and how?

**Round 5: The future**

13. Which way do you want to grow your impact: Size (serve more people), Sustainability (strengthen what you do), Scale (spread your model to others), or Systems Change (change policies or systems)? You can pick one or two. Why?
14. Ten years from now, what will be different for the people you serve, your partners, and your community?

## Step 2: Build a worksheet (for your own use)

Before writing, fill in every framework element with what the user said, using their exact words where possible, and the source (interview answer or document name). Mark any gap `[CONFIRM: what is needed]`. When the interview and a document disagree, ask the user instead of choosing.

## Step 3: Draft the Theory of Change

1. **Cover:** "Theory of Change," organization name, service area, mission in a highlighted box, and "[Month Year]"
2. **Contents**
3. **The Big Idea:** one or two sentences, in their words
4. **The Problem We Exist to Solve:** a short overview, the need in the community (key figures only if each has a confirmed source), root causes, who is most affected, what happens without support
5. **Who We Are and How We Came to Be:** origin story and how the purpose has evolved
6. **Our Theory: How Change Happens:** a table of the pathways, then a short paragraph on how they reinforce each other
7. **One section per pathway:** programs and activities, who we reach, and an outcomes table with Short-Term (6 to 12 months), Intermediate (1 to 3 years), and Long-Term columns
8. **Our Pathway to Impact:** the choice from Round 5 and why, in their words
9. **Consolidated Logic Model:** inputs, activities and outputs by pathway, outcomes by pathway
10. **Measuring Progress:** what they track today, then a short list of suggested indicators clearly labeled "Suggested additions" for them to accept or remove
11. **Our Vision: 10 Years From Now:** grouped by the audiences they named
12. **What Makes This Work: Essential Resources:** including where donor investment matters
13. **Closing line:** a quote from the ED's own interview answers, if one fits. Never write a quote.

If the organization has only one or two programs, use a single pathway and skip the reinforcement paragraph.

## Writing rules

- **Their words first.** The Big Idea, the problem, and the vision should sound like the ED, not like a consultant.
- **Do not add what they did not say.** No programs, pathways, or outcomes they did not raise. Your own ideas go only in "Suggested additions."
- **Separate outputs from outcomes.** Never list a count as an outcome.
- **Statistics need sources.** A number mentioned without a source becomes `[CONFIRM SOURCE: statistic]`.
- **Protect privacy.** No names or identifying details about the people served.
- Plain language and short sentences. Do not use em dashes or the word "just."

## Step 4: Hand it back

1. Create the document named "[Organization] Theory of Change", saved as described in organization-profile, or as a file to download.
2. List every `[CONFIRM]` placeholder and every "Suggested addition" for the user to decide on.
3. Suggest that the ED and board review it, since it describes the whole organization.
4. Once approved, offer to:
   - Add the pathways to Section 2 of the Grant System Profile and the link to Section 4
   - Build the Master Grant Narrative next with master-grant

Change the profile only after the user says yes.
