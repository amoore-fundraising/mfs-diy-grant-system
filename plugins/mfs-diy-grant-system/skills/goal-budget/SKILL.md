# Program and Goal Budget

A goal budget shows what the organization's ideal operation would cost if money were not the limit: market-rate wages, filled positions, and expanded programs. It sits beside the approved operating budget and splits every cost across program areas, Fundraising, and General Admin. It is a planning and donor tool, not an accounting report.

Apply organization-profile and grant-guardrails throughout. Salaries may be sensitive: check the AI policy rules in the profile before asking for them, and use position titles only, never names, in anything donors will see.

This skill always asks, confirms, and then builds. Never build from the first message.

## Step 1: Read what the user shares

Ask for the approved operating budget for this year. Also welcome: a staffing list with titles, FTE, and salaries; any program cost breakdown from the finance person; and notes from a planning conversation about the ideal budget.

Read every file fully, including formulas. Add up the totals yourself and compare them:

- If a program breakdown does not match the approved budget total, it is probably actual spending, not the budget. Say so in plain language, and use it only to estimate how costs split across programs.
- Fundraising and General Admin are often understated in finance files (event costs or consultants missing). Point this out before those percentages are shown to donors.

Build the rows from the budget's own expense categories, plus a position-by-position personnel section.

## Step 2: Ask once, in groups

Ask only what is missing, organized like this:

1. **Program areas:** the program columns to use (usually two to four, from the profile's programs or pathways), and whether any program should have its own separate column.
2. **Staffing:** for each position: goal salary, FTE, and how its time splits across programs, Fundraising, and General Admin. Any new positions in the goal: title, salary, and how many.
3. **Wages:** whether they want help finding market wage data. If yes, use U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics wage data (OEWS) for the state or metro area, cite it with a link, and suggest a percentile with a reason. Never state a wage without a source.
4. **One-time or pending items:** pending grants, new programs, vehicles, buildouts. For each: include it, give it its own column, or leave it in a footnote.
5. **Revenue:** whether they have goal revenue targets, or want yellow placeholders. Note any funding that is ending.
6. **How shared costs are split:** propose shared costs (rent, utilities, insurance, technology) split by each program's share of staff salary dollars; direct costs 100% to their program; special events 50% Fundraising; audit 100% General Admin. Ask them to confirm or change it.
7. **Donor narrative:** whether they want the one-page narrative too.

After they answer, show the full staffing roster as a table with the proposed splits and salary total, list any remaining gaps, and ask: "Want me to go ahead and build?" Build only on a yes.

## Step 3: Build the workbook

Use a spreadsheet skill if one is available. Name the file "[Organization] Program and Goal Budget". Use live formulas, not typed totals.

**Sheet 1: Goal Budget**

- Title rows: organization name, "Program and Goal Budget", date, a one-sentence definition of a goal budget, and a note that yellow cells are for the organization to fill in.
- Columns: Category | [Year] Budget | Goal Budget | one column per program | Fundraising | General Admin | Check
- **Income:** current lines; goal amounts where given, otherwise yellow placeholders; Total Revenue.
- **Personnel:** one row per position title, linked to the Staffing sheet; fringe as rate times salaries; Total Personnel. If the approved budget does not list salaries by position, show current salaries as one total and say so in a cell comment.
- **Non-personnel:** each expense line with current and goal amounts and the split across columns. New goal-only lines (for example, an emergency fund) show $0 current with a comment explaining why.
- **Totals:** Total Expenses, percent of goal expenses for every column, increase over current budget, and the net surplus or gap to fund.
- **Check column:** the rounded difference between the sum of the split columns and the goal amount. It must be zero on every row.
- A notes block at the bottom: how to read the sheet, how shared costs were split, what was excluded, the color key, and wage sources with links.
- Colors: blue for typed inputs, black for formulas, green for links to other sheets, yellow for cells the organization fills in.

**Sheet 2: Staffing**

Position | FTE | Goal Salary | percent to each program, Fundraising, and General Admin | percent check | dollars to each column. Below it: the shared-cost weights (each program's salary dollars divided by total salary dollars) and the fringe rate as one yellow input.

**Sheet 3 (only if needed):** a separate program with its own model, with inputs, formulas, and a total that links to the Goal Budget sheet.

Recalculate and check: no formula errors, every Check cell is zero, and the weights add to 100%. Fix anything that fails before handing it back.

## Step 4: One-page donor narrative (if requested)

A one-page document titled "What It Would Take: Our Goal Budget":

- An opening paragraph: the mission in one sentence, that the current budget is balanced but not enough, and the question the goal budget answers
- Three to five short paragraphs with a bold lead, one per theme (for example: pay staff market wages, reach more people, solve a specific problem, Fundraising and General Admin kept under a stated share)
- A small table of program totals with each one's share of the goal
- A closing paragraph with the goal total, the increase over the current budget, what was excluded, and a sources line

Write in prose, not bullets. Check that it fits on one page.

## Step 5: Hand it back

Save the files as described in organization-profile, or give them as downloads. In the reply, state the current and goal totals, the share for each column, how any market wage was chosen, and what the organization still needs to fill in. Cite all outside data with links.

Offer to add the link to Section 4 of the Grant System Profile, only after the user says yes.

## Rules

- Titles only in anything donors see.
- Never invent goal revenue, salaries, or costs. Use yellow placeholders.
- When two sources disagree on a number, ask. Never choose silently.
- Excluded items get a footnote, not a row.
- This is a planning tool. Accounting, tax, and audit questions go to the organization's accountant.
- Plain language and short sentences. Do not use em dashes or the word "just."
