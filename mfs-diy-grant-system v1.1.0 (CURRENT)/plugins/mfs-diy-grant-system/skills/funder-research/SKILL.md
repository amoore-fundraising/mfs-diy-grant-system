# Funder Research

Help staff find funders worth their time and understand a funder before they start writing.

Apply organization-profile and grant-guardrails throughout.

## Mode A: Find new funders

1. Read organization-profile for programs, service area, and who is served.
2. Ask which program or need the search is for, if the user did not say. Also ask about any timing or grant size that matters.
3. Check the Prospects and Funders lists in the grant tracker first, so you do not suggest funders the organization already tracks.
4. Search for funders that give in the organization's service area and issue area. Good places to look:
   - Local and regional community foundations and their competitive grant programs
   - Local United Way funding rounds
   - Corporate foundations and giving programs of companies with a local presence
   - Family foundations that have funded similar local organizations (from their 990-PF filings, through ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer or Candid)
   - State agency and federal opportunities, only when the organization has the capacity noted in organization-profile
5. Give the user a short list (five to ten), each with the fit rating below and a link to the funder's own page.

## Mode B: Profile one funder

Build a short profile using the funder's own website and guidelines, and its most recent 990-PF if it is a private foundation:

- Mission and focus areas, in the funder's words
- Geography they fund
- Typical grant size (range and median, from the 990-PF where available)
- Who they have funded locally, especially similar organizations
- How to apply: open application, LOI first, or invitation only
- Current deadlines, verified on the funder's own website
- Contact person, if published
- Anything they do not fund

Note the date you checked the deadline and the page you checked it on.

## Fit check

Rate each funder Strong, Possible, or Weak fit, with one or two sentences explaining why. Base it on:

- Does the funder's focus match a program in organization-profile?
- Does the funder give in the organization's service area?
- Does a typical grant fit the request size the organization needs?
- Is the funder open to applications, or invitation only?
- Has the funder supported similar organizations nearby?

This is information, not a decision. The user decides whether to apply. Do not tell them not to apply.

## Saving prospects

Ask before adding anything to the grant tracker. On a yes:

1. Read the Prospects list's columns (see organization-profile).
2. Add one row per funder with the fit rating, focus match, typical grant size, next deadline, and a link to the funder's page.
3. Save a funder profile from Mode B in the grants folder and link it on the row, or add it as an update if the tracker is in monday.com.

When the user decides to apply, hand off to start-grant.
