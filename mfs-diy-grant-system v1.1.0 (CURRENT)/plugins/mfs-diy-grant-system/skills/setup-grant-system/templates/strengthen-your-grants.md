# Ways to Strengthen Your Grants

The Grant System works with whatever you have today. Each of these makes your grants stronger over time. None is required, and you can take them one at a time, in any order.

## AI policy

**What it is:** A short document that says what information your team can and can't put into AI tools, which tools are approved, and that a person reviews everything before it goes out.

**How it helps your grants:** Your team can share documents with confidence, and you have a ready answer when a funder asks how you use AI. More funders are starting to ask.

**A simple first step:** Answer about ten questions, and Claude drafts a policy for your ED or board to adopt. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

**Skill that helps:** ai-policy

## Theory of change

**What it is:** A plain description of the problem you address, what you do about it, and what changes for the people you serve, in the short term and the long term.

**How it helps your grants:** Most funders ask about outcomes. A theory of change gives you clear, consistent answers instead of starting from scratch each time.

**A simple first step:** Set aside an hour with your executive director, and maybe a program lead, to answer a guided set of questions. Claude turns your answers into the document.

**Skill that helps:** theory-of-change

## Master grant

**What it is:** One document that holds everything a grant usually asks for: your organization overview, program descriptions, a needs statement with sourced statistics, outcomes, and the language you prefer.

**How it helps your grants:** Every new grant starts from approved content instead of a blank page. This is the biggest time saver in the system.

**A simple first step:** Upload your annual report and two or three past grants. Claude builds a first draft and marks what is missing.

**Skill that helps:** master-grant

## Program data and stories

**What it is:** A few numbers you track for each program every quarter, such as how many people you served and one change you can show, plus stories that people have agreed you can share.

**How it helps your grants:** Funders want numbers with sources and real stories. Claude never makes these up, so the more you track, the fewer blanks you fill in by hand.

**A simple first step:** Pick one or two numbers per program and write them down each quarter. The Program Data tab in your grant tracker is a simple place to keep them. Ask for written permission before sharing someone's story.

**Skill that helps:** draft-grant-report (to enter quarterly numbers)

## Strategic plan

**What it is:** Your board's plan for where the organization is headed over the next few years.

**How it helps your grants:** Funders like to see that a grant fits a bigger plan. Claude can connect each request to your goals.

**A simple first step:** If you have a plan, even an older one, upload it. If you don't, a strategic planning process with your board is the place to start. This is usually led by your board and often a facilitator.

**Skill that helps:** None. Your board leads this. Claude can help you organize what you have.

## Program budget

**What it is:** Your organization budget split by program, so you can show what each program costs.

**How it helps your grants:** Many funders ask for a program budget. Without one, you can still estimate a fair share of costs for each program based on staff time.

**A simple first step:** For a grant, Claude can help you estimate a program budget from your organization budget using how staff spend their time. Your bookkeeper does not need to change anything for this.

**Going further:** Tracking costs by program in your books takes a conversation with your bookkeeper. A nonprofit-savvy advisor can help translate what funders need into what the bookkeeper sets up.

**Skill that helps:** grant-budget

## Goal budget

**What it is:** Your current budget side by side with an ideal budget: what it would cost to pay staff fair wages, fill open positions, and serve everyone who needs you.

**How it helps your grants:** It shows donors and funders what full funding would make possible, and it guides how much to ask for.

**A simple first step:** This works best after you have a program budget. Many organizations build it with an advisor and their finance person.

**Skill that helps:** goal-budget
