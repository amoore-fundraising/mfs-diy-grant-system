# Award and Stewardship

Carry a grant from submission through closeout, so nothing promised to a funder is forgotten.

Apply organization-profile and grant-guardrails throughout.

## Core rules

- **The award documents govern.** Work from the actual award letter, agreement, and funder guidance, not the proposal alone.
- **Never invent terms.** Never guess report dates, allowable costs, match, payment terms, or closeout deadlines. If the source is unclear, flag it.
- **People approve.** Accepting, signing, and certifying are for the person named in organization-profile. Funder emails are saved as drafts, never sent.
- Log each step as a note on the grant's row on the Grant Calendar, after the user says yes.

## Part 1: Submitted

Log on the grant's row: date submitted, who submitted, confirmation number if given, link to the final package, and when a decision is expected (only if the funder says). Ask whether to change the status.

## Part 2: Decision

- **Declined:** log the date and any feedback. Offer to draft a short, gracious note thanking the funder and asking whether they would share feedback or be open to a future request.
- **Awarded:** go to Part 3.

## Part 3: Award review

When the award letter or agreement arrives, write a short brief covering only what the documents say:

- Amount and grant period
- Approved activities and any differences from the request (amount, scope, timeline)
- Reporting requirements and due dates
- Payment terms
- Restrictions, match, and what needs funder approval to change
- Acknowledgment or publicity requirements
- Closeout and record-keeping requirements
- Open questions, as `[CONFIRM: ...]`
- Who signs (from organization-profile)

Legal, tax, or audit questions in the agreement go to the organization's professional. Flag them.

## Part 4: Grant Commitments Sheet

Once the award is accepted, fill in a Grant Commitments Sheet for the grant, from the proposal and the award documents. Use the organization's own template if the profile links one; otherwise use `templates/grant-commitments-sheet.md` in this skill's folder. Save it as described in organization-profile:

- Every promised output and outcome, worded as in the proposal
- Every budget line
- Every report and its due date
- Acknowledgment requirements

Offer to add report due dates to the Grant Calendar and to update the funder on the Funders list. Make each change only after the user says yes.

## Part 5: Thank you

Draft a short thank-you from the ED to the funder: thanks, what the grant will make possible, and a line about looking forward to sharing results. Keep it to a few sentences. Save it as an email draft, or give it in the chat.

## Part 6: Changes during the grant

When plans change (staffing, timeline, budget, scope, numbers served), write a short change record: what changed, what it affects, which award terms it touches, and whether funder approval appears to be required, citing the term. If the terms are unclear, never conclude approval is not needed. Offer to draft a request to the funder.

## Part 7: Renewal

When a renewal is coming up, start from what actually happened: results against the Commitments Sheet, reporting status, funder feedback, and the funder's current guidelines. Then hand off to start-grant.

## Part 8: Closeout

As the grant ends, list the final reports and due dates, final payments, open conditions, and record-keeping steps, from the award documents only. Never make up a deadline or retention period. Hand the final report to draft-grant-report.
