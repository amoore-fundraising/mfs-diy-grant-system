## 1. Ethos

At [Organization Name], we use artificial intelligence (AI) to support our mission of [mission statement]. AI helps us work more efficiently and spend more time on what matters most: our relationships with the people we serve, our donors, and our community.

Technology should serve people. AI supports our team's judgment and never replaces it. We use AI with human oversight to protect accuracy, empathy, and trust.

This policy applies to all staff, [volunteers, board members, and contractors] who use AI in their work for [Organization Name].

## 2. How we think about AI risk

We take concerns about AI seriously. The long-term debate about where AI is headed is real, and experts disagree about it. What we can control is how we use AI today.

Our commitments:

- People stay in charge of decisions.
- We start small, test carefully, and expand only when a tool has earned our trust.
- We stop using a tool if something feels wrong, and we talk about it openly.
- We are honest with the people we serve, our donors, and our funders about how we use AI.

## 3. The Safe Path

You do not need to memorize this whole policy. If you follow these rules, you are within policy.

1. **Use only approved tools for work that involves organizational information.** See Section 6.
2. **In any other tool, use the annual report test.** Only use information you would be comfortable seeing in our annual report or on our website.
3. **Only put information about individual people into AI** (donors, the people we serve, staff, volunteers, or board members) **in approved tools, and only when this policy allows it.** If you are unsure, ask [Approver] first.
4. **AI can draft, but a person approves before anything goes out.** Nothing is sent, posted, or submitted to someone outside [Organization Name] until a person has read and approved it.
5. **If you would not do it yourself, do not ask AI to do it.**
6. **If you are unsure, ask before, not after.**

## 4. How we use AI

AI may be used for:

- Drafting and editing communications, proposals, and reports.
- Research using publicly available information.
- Analyzing organizational data within the rules in Section 5.
- Meeting notes, project management, and routine administrative tasks.
- [Other approved uses.]

AI will not be used for: [uses the organization has ruled out, for example making decisions about program eligibility or writing to people we serve without staff review].

## 5. Data tiers

We sort information into four tiers. The tier decides which tools can be used and what approval is needed.

- **Green:** public information, our published materials, and sample or made-up data. Any tool.
- **Yellow:** internal information such as budgets, program data, outcomes, and summary giving trends. Approved tools only.
- **Red:** identifiable donor records, individual salaries, and personal information about staff, volunteers, or board members. Approved tools only, for approved purposes. Remove any details the task does not need.
- **Never:** passwords and credentials, bank account and ID numbers, and [case-level information about the people we serve, such as health, court, or school records].

*Template note: organizations serving children, working with courts, or handling health information should place all participant information in "Never" unless a qualified expert has reviewed the tools and uses involved.*

Paying for a tool does not by itself make it safe. What matters is whether the tool can use our information to train its AI, who can see our information, how long it is kept, and whether we used only the information the task needed.

## 7. Levels of AI autonomy

- **Level 1, thinking partner:** chat, analysis, and drafting. Anyone, within the data tiers.
- **Level 2, reads our systems:** AI connected to email, documents, or databases to read information. Anyone using approved tools, within the data tiers.
- **Level 3, changes things with approval:** AI that edits documents or records after a person approves. Set up only with approval from [Approver or approvers].
- **Level 4, acts externally:** AI that sends emails, posts updates, submits forms, or contacts donors, funders, or partners. Set up only with approval from [Approver or approvers], always with a person approving each action, and always tested before use.

*Template note: many organizations make Level 3 and Level 4 approvals a joint decision between two people.*

## 8. Approval before anything goes out

Before AI sends, posts, schedules, or submits anything someone outside [Organization Name] will see, a person must read the complete draft, confirm who it is going to and where it will appear, and approve it. Revised drafts need approval again, and each message is approved separately.

## 9. Experimenting with AI

- Anyone may experiment at Levels 1 and 2 with Green information.
- Yellow and Red information are used only in approved tools.
- New Level 3 or 4 uses are tested first with sample data, then piloted on a small scale before wider use.
- New uses are recorded in [the AI experiment log, location]: what was tried, the tool, the data tier, and the result.

## 10. Testing new tools

A new tool moves through four stages, and [Approver or approvers] approve each move:

1. **Sandbox:** sample or Green data only.
2. **Internal pilot:** organizational work for a few weeks.
3. **Limited use:** real work on a small scale.
4. **Approved:** added to Section 6 with the data tiers it is cleared for.

**We look for:** a clear privacy policy and no training on our data; the ability to limit access, see what the tool did, and require approval; accurate results on a task with a known answer; setup and use without programming; fit with our existing systems; time saved that outweighs cost; and the ability to export our data.

**A tool is ruled out if it:** trains on our data with no opt-out, needs broad access it does not require, hides what it did, needs programming to maintain, cannot delete our data, or comes from a vendor we cannot identify or reach.

## 13. Ethical and legal use

**AI does not change what is allowed.** If you would not do it yourself, do not ask AI to do it. You are responsible for anything AI does at your direction.

We do not use AI to:

- Invent data, outcomes, or statistics.
- Deceive or pressure donors, or create misleading content.
- Impersonate a real person.
- Gather personal information in ways that break a website's terms or someone's privacy.
- Get around an AI tool's built-in safeguards.
- Make decisions about people without human review.

## 14. Accuracy, authenticity, and fairness

- Every statistic and factual claim has a confirmed source, or it is marked as unknown.
- We never invent or combine stories about the people we serve. Stories are shared only with consent.
- We never present an AI-generated image as a real person we serve.
- A person who knows our work reviews and revises AI drafts so they reflect our real voice.
- We review AI work for bias as well as accuracy, especially in language about the communities we serve.

## 15. Security

- Passwords and credentials are stored only in [password manager] and are never entered into AI tools or shared by email.
- AI tools that read email, websites, or documents can be tricked by hidden instructions. This is one reason AI access is limited and actions need approval.
- We verify any request for money, credentials, or urgent changes by calling a known phone number.
- We use a team safe word to confirm unusual requests. It is shared in person or by phone only, never written down in email, documents, or any AI tool, and it is changed if it may have been exposed or when someone leaves.
- Two-factor sign-in is turned on for [key systems].

## 16. Mistakes and incidents

If information goes to the wrong place, an AI error reaches someone outside the organization, or a tool behaves in a concerning way:

1. Tell [Contact] the same day.
2. Decide together how and when to inform anyone affected, and inform them promptly.
3. Fix the problem and take steps to prevent it from happening again.
4. Record what happened in [the AI experiment log].

Reporting a mistake quickly is always the right thing to do.

## 17. Keeping our skills sharp

AI supports our judgment. It does not replace knowing our work. Staff are expected to understand their work well enough to evaluate and improve what AI produces, and [Organization Name] provides training on using AI well and safely.
